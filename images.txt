Every image is a RAW, CANDID DOCUMENTARY PHOTOGRAPH —
indistinguishable from a real behind-the-scenes unit-photographer
still taken on the physical set of a big-budget historical drama
(think Wolf Hall, The Favourite, The Last Duel). This is explicitly
NOT an illustration, painting, matte painting, concept art, fantasy
book cover, or digital art of any kind — if it looks painted,
stylized, or "illustrated" in any way, it has failed the brief. Shot
on a real camera (a Sony A7S III or Canon EOS R5 with a 35–85mm prime
lens), natural or practical light only, visible sensor grain,
individual skin pores, flyaway hairs, fabric weave under close
inspection, and the slightly imperfect framing of real on-set
photography rather than a composed painting. Every character is played
by a South Asian actor with a warm West Bengal complexion and features
— this applies across every play regardless of its nominal setting
(Rome, Venice, Denmark, England, Illyria, an enchanted island, etc.).
Attire, armor, crowns, and props stay exactly as original to the
period/setting of each play — only the cast's look and complexion is
Bengali; nothing about costume or setting changes. Practical lighting
(torches, candlelight, storm light, daylight — whatever the scene
calls for), visible skin texture, fabric weave, weathered props and
set dressing, shallow depth of field, shot on what should read as a
35mm or medium-format film camera. Each prompt depicts ONE famous,
canonical scene of the play — a textbook tableau a reader would
recognize instantly — populated with its named principal characters in
their traditional dramatic configuration, not a generic mood shot. The
two images per play are visually and emotionally distinct scenes that
together sum up the play's arc. No text, no titles, no captions, no
illustration or painterly style, no fantasy-art gloss — it must look
like a genuine photograph of a real moment on a real set. Vertical
